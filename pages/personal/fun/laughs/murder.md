Subject: Interesting Murder Story
&gt;&gt;Date: Wed, 16 Sep 1998 10:56:53 +0530
&gt;&gt;
&gt;&gt;At the 1994 annual awards dinner given for Forensic Science,  AAFS
&gt;&gt;president Dr. Don Harper Mills astounded his audience with the legal
&gt;&gt;complications of a  bizarre death.  Here is the story:
&gt;&gt;On March 23, 1994 the medical examiner viewed the body of Ronald
&gt;&gt;Opus
&gt;&gt;and  concluded that he died from a shotgun wound to the head.  Mr.
&gt;&gt;Opus had jumped  from the top of a ten story building intending to
&gt;&gt;commit suicide.  He  left a note to that effect indicating his
&gt;&gt;despondency.
&gt;&gt;As he fell past the ninth floor his life was interrupted by a shotgun
&gt;&gt;blast  passing through a window which killed him instantly.  Neither
&gt;&gt;the  shooter  nor the decedent was aware that a safety net had been
&gt;&gt;installed just  below  at the eighth floor level to protect some
&gt;&gt;building workers and that  Ronald  Opus would not have been able to
&gt;&gt;complete his suicide the way he had planned.
&gt;&gt;&quot;Ordinarily,&quot; Dr. Mills continued, &quot;A person who sets out to commit
&gt;&gt;suicide  and ultimately succeeds even though the mechanism might not
&gt;&gt;be what  he  intended&quot; is still defined as committing suicide.  That
&gt;&gt;Mr. Opus was shot on the way to certain death nine stories below at
&gt;&gt;street level probably  would not have been successful because of the
&gt;&gt;safety net, caused the medical examiner to  feel that he had a
&gt;&gt;homicide on his hands.
&gt;&gt;The room on the ninth floor whence the shotgun blast emanated was
&gt;&gt;occupied  by an elderly man and his wife. They were arguing
&gt;&gt;vigorously, and he was threatening her with a shotgun. The man was so
&gt;&gt;upset that when he pulled the trigger he completely missed his wife
&gt;&gt;and the pellets went  through the window striking Mister Opus. When
&gt;&gt;one intends to kill  subject A but kills subject B in the attempt, one
&gt;&gt;is guilty of the murder of  subject B.
&gt;&gt;When confronted with the murder charge the old man and his wife were
&gt;&gt;both  adamant. They both said they thought the shotgun was unloaded.
&gt;&gt;The old man said it was his long standing habit to threaten his wife
&gt;&gt;with  the unloaded shotgun.  He had no intention to murder her.
&gt;&gt;Therefore  the  killing of  Mr. Opus appeared to be an accident; that 
is, the gun had  been  accidentally loaded.
&gt;&gt;The continuing investigation turned up a witness who saw the old
&gt;&gt;couple&#x27;s son  loading the shotgun about six weeks prior to the fatal
&gt;&gt;accident.  It  transpired that the old lady had cut off her son&#x27;s
&gt;&gt;financial support and the son, knowing the propensity of his father to
&gt;&gt;use the shotgun  threateningly,  loaded the gun with the expectation
&gt;&gt;that his father would shoot his mother.
&gt;&gt;The case now becomes one of murder on the part of the son for the
&gt;&gt;death of Ronald Opus.
&gt;&gt;Now comes the exquisite twist.  Further investigation revealed that
&gt;&gt;the son was in fact Ronald Opus. He had become increasingly
&gt;&gt;despondent
&gt;&gt; over the failure of his attempt to engineer his mother&#x27;s murder. This
&gt;&gt;led him to jump off the ten story building on March 23rd, only to be
&gt;&gt;killed by a shotgun  blast passing through the ninth story window.
&gt;&gt;The son had actually  murdered himself so the medical examiner closed 
the case as a suicide.  Very  tidy of  him.
&gt;&gt;(A true story from Associated Press, by Kurt Westervelt)
